▪ THE STORY SO FAR

A Short History

1972
Home consoles arrive. The screen is a grid of glowing squares — designers learn to think in pixels.
1985
8-bit golden age. With a handful of colors and 8×8 tiles, artists invent an entire visual language of readable, iconic sprites.
1994
16-bit peak. Bigger palettes and dithering push pixel art to painterly heights before 3D takes the spotlight.
2010+
The indie revival. Pixel art returns as a deliberate aesthetic choice — nostalgia fused with modern design.
NOW
A thriving craft: games, avatars, animation, and showcases exactly like this one.
▪ RULES OF THE GRID

Principles

Constraint is the point. These are the load-bearing rules that make a cluster of squares read as an image.

Limited Palette

Few colors, chosen hard. Restraint forces clarity — each hue earns its place, and the whole reads as one deliberate world.

Dithering

Two colors interleaved in a checker pattern fake a third. It is the pixel artist's gradient — texture and depth without more memory.

Hard Edges

No anti-aliasing by accident. Every boundary is a decision. Clean 1px outlines separate a sprite from its background.

Readability

A sprite must read at 16×16 and from across the room. Silhouette first, detail second. If the shape fails, the pixels fail.

No Stray Pixels

Anti-alias by hand in deliberate clusters. Lone pixels ("jaggies") break the form — group them or remove them.

Respect The Grid

One pixel = one unit. Never scale by fractions. Integer zoom only, or the crispness that defines the whole craft dissolves.

▪ STAFF ROLL

About

A living, single-file showcase of the pixel-art movement — its history, its rules, a gallery of hand-mapped sprites, and a playground where every control actually works.

What This Is

One self-contained HTML file. No build step, no framework, no image assets. Every sprite is a color array painted to canvas; every chart is hand-rolled SVG; every button changes real state.

The Style, Honestly

Two bitmap fonts (Press Start 2P + VT323), a locked palette of six accent colors, hard box-shadow borders as "pixels", CSS dither patterns, and integer-crisp rendering throughout.
